Wake Interference and Heat Transfer of Heated Tandem Triangular Cylinders: Effects of Apex Angle, Gap Ratio, and Reynolds Number

2026 – Present

Independent CFD study · Manuscript submitted

I used transient simulations in ANSYS Fluent to study how the wake of the first heated triangular cylinder changes heat transfer and fluid forces on the second.

At the same width, the 30° triangles transferred the most heat per unit depth. The 90° triangles had the highest average Nusselt number, a dimensionless measure of convective heat transfer. Both rankings held for the upstream and downstream cylinders at a clear gap of three cylinder widths (G/B = 3) and Reynolds number 120. The 30° triangles had more heated surface per unit depth because their perimeter was longer.

Notation: β is the angle at the tip of each triangle (degrees). B is the cylinder width across the flow, fixed at 5 mm. G is the clear gap along the flow, measured from the rear face of the upstream cylinder to the tip of the downstream cylinder. G/B is the gap divided by the width (unitless).

Re is the Reynolds number based on the mean inlet velocity and B. Nu is the dimensionless Nusselt number. Q′ is the heat transfer rate per unit depth (W/m).
