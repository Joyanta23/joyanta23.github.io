Machine learning for aged titanium alloys

2026 – present · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Md. Ashraful Islam

In this project I wanted to see if we can predict the electrical resistivity and hardness of aged titanium alloys just from their composition and heat treatment. There was no ready-made dataset, so I collected the data myself from aging curves in published papers using WebPlotDigitizer. In the end I had 836 resistivity points and 568 hardness points. I tried eight different ML models, and ExtraTrees worked best for both properties. The part I found most interesting was that giving the model the measured resistivity as an extra input made the hardness predictions quite a bit better, with the error dropping by around 21%. My supervisor is reviewing the manuscript now.

Heat transfer around two triangular cylinders in tandem

2026 – present · Ongoing

This is what I'm working on right now. I'm looking at two heated triangular cylinders placed one behind the other in a channel, and how the front one changes the flow and heat transfer for the one behind it. I run 2D transient simulations in ANSYS Fluent and change the apex angle, the gap between the cylinders and the Reynolds number. I first checked my setup against a published single-cylinder case, and the numbers matched well. Some of the cases are still running, so these results are early.

Laminar flow heat transfer with staggered triangular pin fins

2024 – 2025 · Undergraduate thesis · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Md. Shahidul Islam

This was my undergraduate thesis at KUET. I simulated laminar airflow through a staggered array of triangular pin fins in ANSYS Fluent, for Reynolds numbers from 200 to 1000, and compared the results with circular, square, hexagonal and rectangular fins from earlier studies. The triangular fins gave the highest heat transfer, but they also caused a larger pressure drop, so there is a trade-off.
